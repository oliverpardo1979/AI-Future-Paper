# Numerical equilibrium transitions and audits

The finite-terminal equilibrium paths presented in the manuscript are generated by:

```powershell
python scripts/simulate_equilibrium.py
```

This solver treats capital and AI capability as predetermined variables and
consumption and the private shadow value of capability as jump variables. It imposes
monopoly service supply, labor-market clearing, both research first-order conditions,
the developer's costate equation, the household Euler equation, and the aggregate
resource constraint. Its terminal conditions come from the analytical limiting
equilibrium. The reported cases are $\sigma_{XL}=0.75$, the baseline
$\sigma_{XL}=1$, $\sigma_{HM}=2$ path, and the human-essential
$\sigma_{XL}=\sigma_{HM}=1$ benchmark.

`equilibrium_transition_paths.csv` contains the paths and equation residuals;
`equilibrium_transition_summary.csv` reports solver convergence and endpoint
statistics. `equilibrium_research_technology_comparison.png` isolates the effect of
changing $\sigma_{HM}$ at Cobb--Douglas production; the other `equilibrium_*.png`
files retain the production-regime comparison.

Finite-horizon stability is checked with:

```powershell
python scripts/check_equilibrium_horizon_convergence.py
```

The resulting `equilibrium_horizon_convergence.csv` compares the initial jump
variables across 400--900 years for $\sigma_{XL}=0.75$ and 1,000--2,000 years for
both $\sigma_{XL}=1$ research technologies.

The gross-substitution paths are generated by
`scripts/simulate_high_sigma_equilibrium.py`. The script solves a free-boundary
problem: it fixes a large terminal output--capital ratio, treats the time required
to reach it as endogenous, and imposes the singular-equilibrium consumption and
shadow-value ratios derived in the paper. Parameter and boundary continuation are
available through `--previous-path` and `--free-z-sequence`. Saved annual paths are
used only as continuation guesses. The paper-ready files and figures are rebuilt
from fresh collocation solutions with:

```powershell
python scripts/simulate_high_sigma_equilibrium.py --assemble-published
```

`high_sigma_equilibrium_paths.csv` contains the published $\sigma_{XL}=1.35$,
$1.50$, and $2.00$ paths. `high_sigma_equilibrium_summary.csv` reports their
free-boundary and static-equilibrium residuals. The four
`high_sigma_equilibrium_*.png` files report quantities, growth and returns, factor
and research shares, and convergence toward the singular ratios.
`high_sigma_boundary_convergence.csv` records the boundary checks for all three
elasticities: $Y/K=20,50$ for $\sigma_{XL}=1.35$ and 1.50, and $Y/K=20,50,100$
for $\sigma_{XL}=2$.

The published paths are audited equation by equation with:

```powershell
python scripts/audit_equilibrium_outputs.py
```

`equilibrium_system_audit.csv` contains the pointwise diagnostics and
`equilibrium_system_audit_summary.csv` reports the maximum algebraic, dynamic, and
static residuals, the monopoly second-order margin, positivity and interiority
checks, and transversality or singular-endpoint diagnostics. Passing this audit
establishes that a path solves the canonical equilibrium system on its reported
domain. It does not certify the developer's global dynamic optimum when the control
problem is nonconcave, and therefore is not a proof of global existence or
uniqueness.

The repository also retains earlier mechanism exercises generated by:

```powershell
python scripts/simulate_model.py
```

That script requires Python 3.10 or later, NumPy, and Pillow. It:

1. calibrates the constant investment and research-compute shares to the analytical
   Cobb--Douglas balanced-growth path;
2. solves the static monopoly and research-input allocation at every integration
   step;
3. integrates the laws of motion for capital, capability, and population;
4. writes the calibration, scenario summary, and numerical residual checks to this
   directory;
5. searches for real-wage declines across population growth, production
   substitution, and investment shares;
6. traces the analytical automation law and the time-varying feedback denominator
   from an initially human-dominated research sector; and
7. regenerates twelve diagnostic PNG figures, including the complete
   three-scenario diagnostics for aggregate levels, growth rates, input allocation,
   income shares, and returns to capital.

These proportional-allocation files are not equilibrium simulations and are not
evidence in the manuscript. `transition_paths.csv`, `wage_sensitivity_paths.csv`, and
`automation_feedback_paths.csv` are generated locally but ignored by Git because
they are large intermediate files. The wage-path file contains the four detailed
wage experiments. The smaller `wage_sensitivity_summary.csv`,
`wage_decline_grid.csv`, and `wage_sigma_xl_frontier.csv` report the results used in
the text and figures. `labor_share_summary.csv` reports the production and aggregate
labor-income-share comparisons. `research_labor_paths.csv` and
`research_labor_summary.csv` report the transition of $H/M$, $M/N$, and $H/N$ used
to study employment inside the AI-development industry.
`central_transition_summary.csv` reports the turning points and endpoint values for
the central $\sigma_{XL}=0.75$, $1$, and $2$ experiments. The first two paths run
through year 200; the third ends at the acceleration cutoff.
`automation_feedback_summary.csv` reports the relative-cost transition, the
critical automation share, and the singularity date under the reduced
proportional-allocation closure. The committed `validation.csv` reports the maximum
static first-order-condition residuals, the dynamic-identity residuals, the minimum
consumption share, and the Euler gap. The fixed-share closure does not impose the
household Euler equation, so this gap is a diagnostic of the distance from the
optimizing saving path rather than an identity that should equal zero.

Long-horizon falsification exercises are generated by:

```powershell
python scripts/stress_test_regimes.py --horizon 2000 --step 2
```

`regime_stress_summary.csv` reports 29 combinations of production substitution,
research feedback, and initial conditions. `regime_stress_step_convergence.csv`
compares integration steps between half a year and five years. `claim_audit.csv`
maps the paper's principal claims to their propositions, assumptions, proof status,
and remaining gaps. Passing a numerical stress test is not recorded as proof of a
proposition.

These legacy exercises use constant output shares for investment and
automated-research spending. They are not the perfect-foresight solution for
household consumption and the developer's shadow value of capability. In particular,
their high-substitution and wage-decline paths cannot be interpreted as equilibrium
counterexamples. Any such case must be recomputed with an economically justified
terminal condition before it can enter the paper.

# Numerical transition experiments

The experiments in Section 6 are generated by:

```powershell
python scripts/simulate_model.py
```

The script requires Python 3.10 or later, NumPy, and Pillow. It:

1. calibrates the constant investment and research-compute shares to the analytical
   Cobb--Douglas balanced-growth path;
2. solves the static monopoly and research-input allocation at every integration
   step;
3. integrates the laws of motion for capital, capability, and population;
4. writes the calibration, scenario summary, and numerical residual checks to this
   directory;
5. searches for real-wage declines across population growth, production
   substitution, and investment shares;
6. traces the analytical automation law and the time-varying feedback denominator
   from an initially human-dominated research sector; and
7. regenerates the twelve PNG figures used by the paper, including the complete
   three-scenario diagnostics for aggregate levels, growth rates, input allocation,
   income shares, and returns to capital.

`transition_paths.csv`, `wage_sensitivity_paths.csv`, and
`automation_feedback_paths.csv` are generated locally but ignored by Git because
they are large intermediate files. The wage-path file contains the four detailed
wage experiments. The smaller `wage_sensitivity_summary.csv`,
`wage_decline_grid.csv`, and `wage_sigma_xl_frontier.csv` report the results used in
the text and figures. `labor_share_summary.csv` reports the production and aggregate
labor-income-share comparisons. `research_labor_paths.csv` and
`research_labor_summary.csv` report the transition of $H/M$, $M/N$, and $H/N$ used
to study employment inside the AI-development industry.
`central_transition_summary.csv` reports the turning points and endpoint values for
the central $\sigma_{XL}=0.75$, $1$, and $2$ experiments. The first two paths run
through year 200; the third ends at the acceleration cutoff.
`automation_feedback_summary.csv` reports the relative-cost transition, the
critical automation share, and the singularity date under the reduced
proportional-allocation closure. The committed `validation.csv` reports the maximum
static first-order-condition residuals, the dynamic-identity residuals, the minimum
consumption share, and the Euler gap. The fixed-share closure does not impose the
household Euler equation, so this gap is a diagnostic of the distance from the
optimizing saving path rather than an identity that should equal zero.

Long-horizon falsification exercises are generated by:

```powershell
python scripts/stress_test_regimes.py --horizon 2000 --step 2
```

`regime_stress_summary.csv` reports 29 combinations of production substitution,
research feedback, and initial conditions. `regime_stress_step_convergence.csv`
compares integration steps between half a year and five years. `claim_audit.csv`
maps the paper's principal claims to their propositions, assumptions, proof status,
and remaining gaps. Passing a numerical stress test is not recorded as proof of a
proposition.

These experiments use constant output shares for investment and automated-research
spending. They are not the full perfect-foresight solution for household consumption
and the developer's shadow value of capability.

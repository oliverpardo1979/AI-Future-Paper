Artificial Intelligence, Market Structure, and the Dynamics of Research}
\author{Oliver Pardo}

\begin{abstract}
This paper studies how substitution between labor and AI services, and between
human and automated AI research, shapes economic growth, real wages, and labor's
share of output. An integrated monopolist supplies AI services and invests in
improving AI systems. Final output combines capital with a CES aggregate of labor
and AI services, while AI research combines human researchers and automated
research services through a second CES technology. We derive four growth regimes
and compute perfect-foresight equilibrium transitions that illustrate them. When
labor and AI services are complements, labor becomes the long-run bottleneck: any
nondegenerate interior constant-growth limit with positive AI-quality growth has
zero per-capita output growth. With Cobb--Douglas final production, a
human-essential research technology admits a finite constant-growth candidate
under stability and feasibility restrictions. When research inputs are gross
substitutes, the contribution of automated research becomes endogenous and
strengthens the feedback from output to AI research as automation proceeds. When
labor and AI services are gross substitutes, no interior steady state can combine
positive AI-quality growth with finite constant growth rates. Under an additional
AI-dominated scaling, we characterize a conditional finite-time singular path. The
computed equilibrium paths can nevertheless remain close to balanced growth for
centuries before accelerating sharply. Across regimes, real wages can rise while
labor's share falls, and human research employment can initially expand even as its
long-run population share approaches zero.
\end{abstract}

\title{Automating Innovation:\\
Artificial Intelligence, Market Structure, and the Dynamics of Research}
\author{Oliver Pardo}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{abstract}
This paper studies a dynamic economy in which an integrated AI developer supplies
quality-adjusted AI services and uses human and automated research to improve frontier
capability. Research automation is an endogenous transition driven by the relative
cost of human and machine inputs. While human research remains essential, population
growth limits innovation; when automated research becomes dominant, capability growth
feeds back through output and research spending. Human essentiality can therefore
support balanced growth in parameter regions where full research automation generates
acceleration or, under a reduced closure, a finite-time singularity. Production
substitution creates a separate regime distinction: when AI services and labor are
gross complements, labor remains a long-run bottleneck; when they are gross
substitutes and capability is unbounded, the return to capital diverges and no
interior finite-growth steady state exists. Near
unit elasticity, however, the economy can remain close to Cobb--Douglas for an
extremely long transition. Research can also become less human intensive while
employing a larger share of the population, and the wage can rise even as labor's
share falls. Monopoly
restricts current AI deployment but creates rents that finance frontier innovation,
linking access regulation to research policy.
\end{abstract}

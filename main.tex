\begin{abstract}
This paper studies how substitution between labor and AI services, and between
human and automated inputs in AI research, shapes growth, wages, and labor's share. A
monopolist supplies AI services and invests in better AI. Final output combines
capital with a CES aggregate of labor and AI services, while AI research combines
human and automated inputs. If labor and AI services are complements, labor remains
a long-run bottleneck, and output per capita is constant in any interior
constant-growth equilibrium with improving AI. When production and research are
Cobb--Douglas, the model admits a balanced-growth candidate under stability and
feasibility conditions. If labor and AI services are gross substitutes, no interior
steady state combines improving AI with finite constant growth rates; nevertheless,
the economy may remain near balanced growth for a long time before accelerating.
Numerical equilibrium paths show that labor's share can fall while wages rise. With
very high substitution, wages can temporarily fall, but their recovery is not a
general result.
\end{abstract}

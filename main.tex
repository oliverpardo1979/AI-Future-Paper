\title{Automating Innovation:\\
Artificial Intelligence, Market Structure, and the Dynamics of Research}
\author{Oliver Pardo}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This paper studies how automating AI research changes economic growth, wages, labor's
share, and market power. A monopolist sells AI services and reinvests profits in better
AI, using human researchers and AI systems to conduct research. Automation arises as
research performed by AI systems becomes cheaper than research performed by humans. If
human researchers remain indispensable, population growth limits innovation and the
economy may converge to constant growth rates. If AI systems
can largely replace researchers, better AI raises output and research spending, causing
growth to accelerate without bound; in a limiting case, output becomes infinite in finite
time. How easily AI services replace labor creates a second divide. Difficult substitution
makes labor a long-run constraint. Easy substitution, combined with unbounded AI quality,
makes the return to capital rise without limit and rules out constant growth rates. This
divergence can take extremely long to emerge near the Cobb--Douglas benchmark. During the
transition, research can become less human intensive while employing a larger share of the
population, and wages can rise while labor's share falls. Monopoly limits current AI use
but generates profits that finance research, linking competition policy to innovation.
\end{abstract}

\title{Automating Innovation:\\
Artificial Intelligence, Market Structure, and the Dynamics of Research}
\author{Oliver Pardo}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This paper studies how automating AI research affects growth, real wages, labor's share of
output, and the consumption--output ratio. A monopolist supplies AI services and reinvests
rents in better AI. Production and research are CES: the former combines labor and AI
services; the latter combines human and automated inputs. Relative costs determine the
research mix endogenously. In the human-dominated benchmark, population growth limits
innovation. As automation comes to dominate research, output and research spending feed
back into AI improvement and can replace balanced growth with acceleration. The production
elasticity of substitution yields three regimes. Below one, labor is an asymptotic
bottleneck: any nondegenerate interior constant-growth limit has zero per-capita output
growth, and developer optimality requires research inputs to grow more slowly than
population. At one, an AI-dominated constant-growth candidate has positive finite rates
only under stability and resource-feasibility conditions. Above one, unbounded AI
capability makes the return to capital diverge and rules out an interior steady state with
finite rates, although the result may emerge arbitrarily slowly near Cobb--Douglas. More
AI services per worker can lower labor's share of output while real
wages rise; sufficiently strong substitution relative to capital deepening can also lower
wages. Within research, the human-to-machine input ratio can fall even as human research
employment rises as a share of the population. Monopoly restricts current AI use, but its
rents finance research, linking competition and innovation policy.
\end{abstract}

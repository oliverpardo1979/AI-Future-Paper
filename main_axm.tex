\title{The Future of Growth and Human Labor under Automated AI Research:\\
AI Capability as Research-Augmenting Technology}
\author{Oliver Pardo}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This paper develops an endogenous-growth model in which current AI capability
turns compute into both production services and automated research services. An
integrated monopolist supplies AI services and invests in better AI using human
researchers and capability-augmented research compute. Final output combines
capital with a CES aggregate of labor and AI services. When labor and AI services
are gross complements, every regular finite-rate balanced-growth path
characterized here has constant output per person and real wages, while labor
retains a positive income share. Audited finite-horizon paths show that capability
can keep growing and automated services can receive nearly all research
expenditure while these limits emerge. At unit elasticity in final production,
positive per-capita growth under diminishing returns to current research inputs
requires the feedback from better AI to output and research to be bounded; that
restriction alone does not guarantee existence. If labor and AI services are
gross substitutes and AI services come to dominate their composite, unbounded AI
improvement rules out finite constant growth rates. On a conditional branch of
the necessary conditions where output becomes unbounded in finite time, labor's
income share vanishes, but the real wage eventually rises when the smaller of the
two substitution elasticities remains below an explicit threshold. Free-boundary
approximations support this conditional branch without establishing an
infinite-horizon equilibrium.
\end{abstract}

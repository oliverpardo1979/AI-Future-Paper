\title{The Future of Growth and Human Labor under Automated AI Research:\\
AI Capability as Research-Augmenting Technology}
\author{Oliver Pardo}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This paper develops an endogenous-growth model in which current AI capability
turns compute into both production services and automated research services. A
monopolist supplies AI services and invests in better AI using human researchers
and capability-augmented research compute. Final output combines capital with a CES aggregate of labor
and AI services. On any finite-rate balanced-growth path satisfying the conditions
characterized when labor and AI services are gross complements in final
production, labor is a long-run bottleneck:
if production labor remains a positive population share, output per person is
constant. For the interior candidate with unit elasticity in final production,
positive per-capita growth under the maintained diminishing-returns baseline
requires the feedback from better AI to production and research to be bounded;
that restriction alone does not guarantee
existence. If labor and AI services are gross substitutes and AI services come to
dominate the labor--AI composite,
an interior path with unbounded AI improvement cannot have finite constant growth
rates. On a conditional AI-dominated branch of the necessary conditions where
output becomes unbounded in finite time, aggregate labor income as a share of
output vanishes, but real wages
eventually rise when the smaller of the two substitution elasticities remains
below an explicit threshold. Finite-horizon perfect-foresight approximations
illustrate the unit-elasticity transitions. Separately validated free-boundary
calculations produce approximations that converge on common intervals toward a
conditional pre-singular branch under gross substitution, without claiming an
infinite-horizon equilibrium.
\end{abstract}

\section{Baseline model}
\label{sec:axm-model}

Time is continuous. There is one final good, a representative household,
competitive final-good firms, and an integrated AI developer. The developer
monopolistically supplies AI services and invests in better AI. The initial
economy already uses AI services; the model does not describe their first
commercial adoption.

\begin{assumption}[Parameters]
Throughout, \(0<\alpha<1\), \(0<n<\rho\), \(\delta\geq0\),
\(\chi>0\), \(0<\eta<1\), \(\sigma_{XL},\sigma_{HM}>0\), and all CES
weights lie strictly between zero and one and sum to one within each CES
aggregate.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Population and labor endowment}

Population grows exogenously at rate \(n\):
\begin{equation}
 \dot N=nN.
 \label{eq:axm-population}
\end{equation}
Each person supplies one unit of homogeneous labor inelastically, so the
household supplies \(N\) units at the common wage \(w\). Final-good firms demand
production labor \(L\), and the AI developer demands human research labor \(H\).
The equality \(N=L+H\) is a labor-market-clearing condition and is imposed only
when equilibrium is defined. For any positive variable \(V\), write
\(g_V\equiv\dot V/V\).

\subsection{Household}

The representative household takes the paths of the net interest rate \(r\),
the wage \(w\), distributed developer profit \(\Pi\), and population \(N\) as
given. Let \(K\) denote its holdings of physical capital. Given \(K(0)>0\), it
chooses \(C\) and \(K\) to maximize
\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{-\rho t}N_t\log(C_t/N_t)\,\dd t.
\]
It owns physical capital and the AI developer and receives labor income, the net
return on capital, and distributed profit. Its flow budget is
\begin{equation}
 \dot K=rK+wN+\Pi-C.
 \label{eq:axm-household-budget}
\end{equation}
The variable \(r\) is the net real interest rate: depreciation is already
deducted from the rental return to capital. At this stage, \(r\) is a price the
household takes as given; no production-side condition has yet been imposed. The
household's interior Euler equation in aggregate variables is therefore
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dot C}{C}=n+r-\rho.
 \label{eq:axm-euler}
\end{equation}
The infinite-horizon terminal restriction maintained for the household is
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-\rho t}\frac{N_tK_t}{C_t}=0.
 \label{eq:axm-household-tvc}
\end{equation}
The developer's problem below defines \(\Pi\). The equilibrium definition later
requires the household's capital holdings to equal final-good firms' capital
demand and links household labor supply to the two sectors' labor demands.

\subsection{Final-good firms}

Competitive final-good firms take \(r,w,p_X\) as given and demand capital,
production labor, and AI services. To preserve the notation used in the rest of
the paper and in the numerical code, \(K\) and \(X\) are not given agent-specific
superscripts. In this subsection they denote capital and AI services demanded by
the final sector; in the household problem \(K\) denotes capital holdings, and in
the developer's problem \(X\) denotes sales. Equality of the corresponding
quantities is imposed only in equilibrium. The production technology is
\begin{align}
 Y&=K^\alpha Z^{1-\alpha},\\
 Z&=\left[\omega_L L^{\frac{\sigma_{XL}-1}{\sigma_{XL}}}
 +\omega_X X^{\frac{\sigma_{XL}-1}{\sigma_{XL}}}\right]^{
 \frac{\sigma_{XL}}{\sigma_{XL}-1}},
 \qquad \omega_L+\omega_X=1.
 \label{eq:axm-final-production}
\end{align}
At the unit-elasticity parameter limit, \(\sigma_{XL}\to1\), the CES becomes the
Cobb--Douglas aggregate \(Z=L^{\omega_L}X^{\omega_X}\). Define the contribution
of AI services to the CES quantity index as
\begin{equation}
 s_X=\frac{\omega_X X^{(\sigma_{XL}-1)/\sigma_{XL}}}
 {\omega_L L^{(\sigma_{XL}-1)/\sigma_{XL}}
 +\omega_X X^{(\sigma_{XL}-1)/\sigma_{XL}}}.
 \label{eq:axm-sx}
\end{equation}
Thus \(s_X\) is the elasticity of \(Z\) with respect to \(X\). The competitive
sector solves
\begin{equation}
 \max_{K,L,X\geq0}\left\{K^\alpha Z(L,X)^{1-\alpha}
 -(r+\delta)K-wL-p_XX\right\}.
 \label{eq:axm-final-firm-problem}
\end{equation}
The interior first-order conditions are
\begin{equation}
 r=\alpha\frac{Y}{K}-\delta,\qquad
 w=(1-\alpha)(1-s_X)\frac{Y}{L},\qquad
 p_X=(1-\alpha)s_X\frac{Y}{X}.
 \label{eq:axm-factor-prices}
\end{equation}
The final-good firm's AI-demand condition implies that AI-service expenditure
as a share of final output is
\(p_XX/Y=(1-\alpha)s_X\). Equivalently, within total payments to the
noncapital composite, \(p_XX/[(1-\alpha)Y]=s_X\). After AI-service-market
clearing, the first ratio is the developer's gross service revenue as a share of
final output.
Constant returns and the three first-order conditions imply zero profit:
\begin{equation}
 Y=(r+\delta)K+wL+p_XX.
 \label{eq:axm-final-zero-profit}
\end{equation}
Solving the AI-demand condition for its price yields the inverse demand schedule
\(p_X(X;K,L)\). The notation retains the paper's convention: \(p_X\) without
arguments denotes the realized price, whereas \(p_X(X;K,L)\) denotes the inverse
demand function. This schedule comes from final-good firms' optimal demand; it is
not a first-order condition of the developer. Its inverse absolute elasticity is
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_X^{-1}=\frac{1-s_X}{\sigma_{XL}}+\alpha s_X.
 \label{eq:axm-inverse-elasticity}
\end{equation}

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{remark}[Tasks, jobs, and labor services]
The inputs \(L\) and \(H\) are quantities of homogeneous labor services, not
numbers of tasks, occupations, or jobs. A job can combine several tasks, and AI
can replace some of them without eliminating the job. The model abstracts from
that margin. In the equilibrium defined below, labor-market clearing imposes
full employment: people can move between final production and AI research, but
the model contains neither unemployment nor job creation and destruction.
Consequently, \(\sigma_{XL}\)
measures substitution between aggregate labor and AI services in final
production. It is not a task-level elasticity or a direct measure of the number
of jobs displaced by AI. The model's labor-market predictions concern wages,
factor shares, and the allocation of labor between the two sectors.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Integrated AI developer}

\subsubsection{AI-service technology}

The model distinguishes two uses of compute. \(U\) is inference compute: raw
compute used to operate existing AI systems and supply services to final-good
firms. \(M\) is AI R\&D compute---called research compute below---used to
improve AI capability. We use the term in the broad training-and-R\&D sense of
\citet{korinekmckelvey2026}: it includes pretraining, post-training,
experimentation, synthetic-data generation, automated algorithmic search, and
compute used by AI systems engaged in research. The two inputs are flows, not
stocks of chips. They have the same constant unit resource cost. We choose units
of raw compute so that one unit costs one unit of final output.

\(A\) indexes current AI capability: what one unit of compute can accomplish.
Inference compute produces the flow of quality-adjusted AI services supplied by
the developer,
\begin{equation}
 X=AU.
 \label{eq:axm-ai-service}
\end{equation}
Research compute analogously produces the automated research service flow
\(AM\), which enters the research technology below. The paid inputs are \(U\)
and \(M\), whereas \(X\) and \(AM\) are service flows. This product of nonrival
capability and rival compute is the one-capability version of the AI-service
accounting in \citet{davidsonetal2026}.

The common unit cost is a normalization, not an assumption that compute is
economically free. To see this, start from a common cost \(\xi>0\), define
\(\widetilde U=\xi U\), \(\widetilde M=\xi M\),
\(\widetilde A=A/\xi\), and then use the normalized variables. Both service
flows and all resource costs are unchanged. The corresponding rescaling of
research productivity is stated after the capability law. Producing one unit of
either service flow---one unit of \(X\) or of \(AM\)---therefore uses \(1/A\)
units of the final good.

technology. It separates raw inference compute, the AI services purchased by
final-good firms, and the service composite used in production. The two
technology diagrams in this subsection are schematics, not numerical results.
Gray boxes denote current inputs, blue boxes denote stocks, and orange boxes
denote transformations or outputs.

\subsubsection{Frontier research technology}

For \(\sigma_{HM}\neq1\), human researchers and automated research services
improve capability through the homothetic CES technology
\begin{equation}
 \dot A=\chi
 \left[\omega_H H^{\varrho_{HM}}
 +\omega_M(AM)^{\varrho_{HM}}\right]^{\eta/\varrho_{HM}},
 \qquad
 \varrho_{HM}=\frac{\sigma_{HM}-1}{\sigma_{HM}},
 \qquad \omega_H+\omega_M=1.
 \label{eq:axm-capability-law}
\end{equation}
Under the compute-unit normalization described above, the law for
\(\widetilde A\) has the same form with
\(\widetilde\chi=\chi/\xi\). Relabeling the normalized variables therefore
preserves the technology without introducing another economic assumption.
For its dual representation, let \(E\) denote the constant-returns
effective-research index
\begin{equation}
 E\equiv\left[\omega_H H^{\varrho_{HM}}
 +\omega_M(AM)^{\varrho_{HM}}\right]^{1/\varrho_{HM}}.
 \label{eq:axm-effective-research}
\end{equation}
The object \(E\) is an index of the two research inputs, not an additional good,
stock, or production stage. The capability law is equivalently
\(\dot A=\chi E^\eta\); hence a target \(\dot A\) requires
\(E=(\dot A/\chi)^{1/\eta}\). At the unit-elasticity parameter limit,
\(\sigma_{HM}\to1\), the technology becomes
\begin{equation}
 \dot A=\chi H^{\eta\omega_H}(AM)^{\eta\omega_M},
 \qquad E=H^{\omega_H}(AM)^{\omega_M}.
 \label{eq:axm-capability-law-cd}
\end{equation}

The parameters have separate economic roles. The elasticity \(\sigma_{HM}\)
governs substitution between human research services \(H\) and automated
research services \(AM\). The exponent \(\eta\) governs returns to their joint
scale: for a fixed \(A\), multiplying both \(H\) and \(M\) by \(\lambda\)
multiplies \(\dot A\) by \(\lambda^\eta\). The scalar \(\chi\) shifts research
productivity and affects the level and timing of capability improvement.
Finally, \(A\) enters inside \(AM\) because capability raises the automated
research services produced by one unit of research compute. A high substitution
elasticity therefore does not remove decreasing returns when \(0<\eta<1\).

Figure~\ref{fig:axm-research-flow} makes the new research specification
explicit. Capability does not enter \(\dot A\) through a separate stock term.
It raises the automated research services produced by the paid input \(M\).

The baseline contains no additional \(A^\phi\) term. Better capability affects
research only by raising the services produced by \(M\). Following the distinction
in \citet{jones1995,davidsonetal2026}, \(\eta\) is an intratemporal scale parameter,
not an intertemporal idea-difficulty parameter. A separate term \(A^{1-b}\) would
add that second margin. Holding the effective-research index fixed, it contributes
the factor \(A^{-b}\) to capability growth, so any \(b>0\) introduces
intertemporal diminishing returns; if \(1-b>0\), the stock also has a positive
level effect on \(\dot A\). The baseline imposes \(b=1\), removes the separate
level-stock term, and isolates research automation.

\subsubsection{Dynamic problem}

The developer sells \(X\), pays \(X/A\) for inference compute, hires \(H\), uses
\(M\) internally, and owns \(A\). It faces the inverse demand schedule derived
from final-good firms' optimal demand. Labor-market clearing is imposed in
equilibrium, but the developer does not internalize its effect on the competitive
wage. Taking the paths of \(r,w,K,\) and \(L\) as given, it solves
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\{X,H,M\}}
 \int_0^\infty
 \exp\left\{-\int_0^t r_s\dd s\right\}
 \left[p_X(X_t;K_t,L_t)X_t-\frac{X_t}{A_t}-w_tH_t-M_t\right]\dd t.
 \label{eq:axm-developer-problem}
\end{equation}
subject to the capability law and the initial capability stock. This is the
developer's primitive optimization problem. The discount rate is the market
return available to the household that owns the developer. At this stage, the
developer takes that price path as given. Equilibrium later requires consistency
between this discount factor, household saving, and the return generated by
final-good firms. The developer's instantaneous distributed profit is
\begin{equation}
 \Pi=p_X(X;K,L)X-\frac{X}{A}-wH-M
 =p_XX-U-wH-M.
 \label{eq:axm-developer-profit}
\end{equation}

The three controls do not play the same intertemporal role. Sales \(X\) do not
enter the capability law or any other intertemporal constraint, and the developer
faces no firm-level capacity constraint linking inference compute \(U\) to
research compute \(M\). Human research \(H\) and research compute \(M\), by
contrast, change future capability through \(\dot A\). The full problem can
therefore be decomposed into a pointwise operating choice and a conditional
within-date allocation of research inputs. The scale of research remains a
dynamic choice. These decompositions belong to the developer's single
optimization problem; they are not additional equilibrium conditions.

\subsubsection{Within-date AI-service supply}

Conditional on current \(A,K,\) and \(L\), the choice of \(X\) is pointwise.
The developer chooses a sales quantity along the inverse demand schedule; this
uses the buyer's demand relation inside the seller's problem, but it is not the
developer's own demand condition. Optimized operating profit before research
expenditure is
\begin{equation}
 \pi(A;K,L)=\max_{X\geq0}
 \left\{p_X(X;K,L)X-\frac{X}{A}\right\}.
 \label{eq:axm-service-supply}
\end{equation}
The residual-demand constraint requires the quantity offered to equal the
quantity demanded at its associated transaction price. General equilibrium
below records this transaction-level condition together with capital-, labor-,
and goods-market clearing. On an interior monopoly branch, the first-order
condition is
\begin{equation}
 p_X(1-\varepsilon_X^{-1})=\frac{1}{A}.
 \label{eq:axm-monopoly-foc}
\end{equation}
Writing \(e_X=\varepsilon_X^{-1}\), the strict second-order condition for the
within-date supply choice is
\begin{equation}
 e_X(1-e_X)
 +(\alpha-\sigma_{XL}^{-1})(1-\sigma_{XL}^{-1})s_X(1-s_X)>0.
 \label{eq:axm-monopoly-soc}
\end{equation}
The numerical solver checks this inequality at every reported date.

Whenever the pointwise maximum in \eqref{eq:axm-service-supply} is attained,
substituting optimized operating profit into
\eqref{eq:axm-developer-problem} gives the equivalent reduced problem
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\{H,M\}}
 \int_0^\infty
 \exp\left\{-\int_0^t r_s\dd s\right\}
 \left[\pi(A_t;K_t,L_t)-w_tH_t-M_t\right]\dd t,
 \label{eq:axm-developer-problem-reduced}
\end{equation}
subject to the same capability law and initial stock. Thus the maximization over
\(X\) in \eqref{eq:axm-service-supply} is the pointwise inner maximization of
the dynamic problem, not a second decision problem. Along the optimum,
\(\Pi_t=\pi(A_t;K_t,L_t)-w_tH_t-M_t\).

Figure~\ref{fig:axm-developer-decomposition} summarizes this sequence. It does
not add an agent or an equilibrium condition; it only displays two equivalent
representations of the same optimization problem.

\subsubsection{Conditional research cost and dynamic optimality}

The mix of \(H\) and \(M\) can also be characterized within date. Conditional
on current \(A\) and \(w\), the unit-expenditure problem for the
effective-research index is
\begin{equation}
 P_E(A,w)=\min_{H,M\geq0}\left\{wH+M:
 E(H,AM)\geq1\right\}.
 \label{eq:axm-research-cost-problem}
\end{equation}
This is the dual representation of the research-input choice inside
\eqref{eq:axm-developer-problem-reduced}, not a separate equilibrium condition.
For \(\sigma_{HM}\neq1\), its solution is
\begin{equation}
 P_E=\left[\omega_H^{\sigma_{HM}}w^{1-\sigma_{HM}}
 +\omega_M^{\sigma_{HM}}\left(\frac{1}{A}\right)^{1-\sigma_{HM}}
 \right]^{1/(1-\sigma_{HM})}.
 \label{eq:axm-research-price}
\end{equation}
At the unit-elasticity parameter limit, its continuous limit is
\begin{equation}
 P_E=\left(\frac{w}{\omega_H}\right)^{\omega_H}
 \left(\frac{1}{A\omega_M}\right)^{\omega_M}.
 \label{eq:axm-research-price-cd}
\end{equation}
Conditional on current \(A\) and \(w\), the minimum expenditure required to
produce a target flow \(\dot A\) is
\begin{equation}
 P_E\left(\frac{\dot A}{\chi}\right)^{1/\eta}.
 \label{eq:axm-research-expenditure-function}
\end{equation}
Thus \(P_E\) is not the unit cost of \(\dot A\) when \(\eta<1\).
Conditional input demands are
\begin{equation}
 H=\omega_H^{\sigma_{HM}}\left(\frac{P_E}{w}\right)^{\sigma_{HM}}E,
 \quad
 AM=\omega_M^{\sigma_{HM}}(AP_E)^{\sigma_{HM}}E.
 \label{eq:axm-research-demands}
\end{equation}
The share of research expenditure allocated to automated research services is
\begin{equation}
 s_M=\frac{M}{wH+M}.
 \label{eq:axm-sm}
\end{equation}
Because producing \(AM\) costs \(M\), the conditional demands imply that, along
an interior cost-minimizing research allocation, \(s_M\) is also the elasticity
of \(E\) with respect to \(AM\). It follows that the elasticity of \(\dot A\)
with respect to \(AM\) is \(\eta s_M\). Holding \(H\) and \(M\) fixed, this is
also the local elasticity of \(\dot A\) with respect to the predetermined
capability stock \(A\). These equalities use interior cost minimization; \(s_M\)
is not a physical fraction of research tasks performed by AI. More generally,
\(s_X=\partial\ln Z/\partial\ln X\) follows directly from the
final-production CES. The equality
\(s_M=\partial\ln E/\partial\ln(AM)\) combines the research CES with interior
conditional cost minimization. Evaluated along an interior equilibrium path,
both are endogenous local feedback weights of the kind emphasized by
\citet{davidsonetal2026}. Unlike their fractions of tasks already automated, our
weights are not task shares.

If the capability law contained the additional term \(A^{1-b}\), then, along
the interior cost-minimizing allocation, the net local elasticity would be
\(\partial\ln g_A/\partial\ln A\vert_{H,M}=\eta s_M-b\). Thus the separate
ideas-difficulty force need not dominate the automation feedback.

Let \(q\) be the current-value shadow price of capability. Under the compute-unit
normalization described above, its rescaled counterpart is
\(\widetilde q=\xi q\); the untilded \(q\) below is the normalized shadow price
used throughout the analysis and simulations. The direct first-order conditions
for the two research inputs are
\begin{equation}
 q\frac{\partial\dot A}{\partial H}=w,\qquad
 q\frac{\partial\dot A}{\partial M}=1.
 \label{eq:axm-research-focs}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, combining the conditional input demands with the optimal choice of
the scale \(E\) gives
\begin{equation}
 q\chi\eta E^{\eta-1}=P_E.
 \label{eq:axm-research-scale-foc}
\end{equation}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
The pointwise operating choice and the conditional research-input allocation
provide the derivatives needed for the dynamic condition. On a differentiable
interior maximizing branch, the envelope theorem applied to
\eqref{eq:axm-service-supply} gives \(\pi_A=X/A^2\), where \(X\) is the selected
sales quantity. Along the interior cost-minimizing research allocation,
\(\partial\dot A/\partial A=\eta s_M\dot A/A\), because a fixed \(M\) supplies
more research services when \(A\) rises. The current-value costate equation is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dot q}{q}=r-\frac{X}{qA^2}
 -\eta s_M\frac{\dot A}{A}.
 \label{eq:axm-costate}
\end{equation}
This is the developer's condition in terms of the market return it takes as
given. The last term is the main dynamic difference from research that combines
\(H\) directly with \(M\). The infinite-horizon terminal restriction maintained
for the developer is
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{t\to\infty}
 \exp\left\{-\int_0^t r_s\dd s\right\}q_tA_t=0.
 \label{eq:axm-developer-tvc}
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[The no-AI boundary]
The no-AI economy is a useful boundary case, although it lies outside the
maintained interior-weight assumption. If \(\omega_X=\omega_M=0\), then
\(Z=L\) and capability has no productive use. Once the developer's problem is
imposed, capability therefore has no market value and an optimal developer
conducts no research. The model reduces to a Ramsey--Cass--Koopmans economy with
population growth and no technological progress.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The constant-returns boundary]
\label{rem:axm-eta-one}
At \(\eta=1\), doubling \(H\) and \(M\) for a fixed capability stock doubles
\(\dot A\). If, in addition, \(\sigma_{HM}>1\) and \(AM/H\to\infty\), then
\[
 \frac{\dot A}{A}
 \sim \chi\,\omega_M^{1/\varrho_{HM}}M.
\]
In this technological limit, a higher level of research compute raises the
growth rate of capability. This scaling does not by itself establish which
equilibrium branch is reached or whether output becomes singular because \(M\)
is endogenous. Moreover, the scale condition just derived becomes
\(q\chi=P_E\) and no longer determines \(E\). If \(q\chi<P_E\), the developer
chooses no research at the margin; if \(q\chi>P_E\), its unconstrained problem
has no finite interior research choice. We maintain \(0<\eta<1\) in the
baseline to distinguish substitution from research-scale effects. The boundary
\(\eta=1\) requires a separate analysis of interiority, existence,
transversality, and the asymptotic equilibrium.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Equilibrium}

Capital-market clearing requires the household's capital holdings to equal the
capital rented by final-good firms. AI-service-market clearing requires the
quantity purchased by final-good firms to equal the quantity sold by the
developer at the transaction price \(p_X\). These conditions identify the
quantities denoted by \(K\) and \(X\) in the separate agent problems. From this
point onward, \(K\) and \(X\) denote their common equilibrium values. Labor-market
clearing imposes
\begin{equation}
 N=L+H.
 \label{eq:axm-labor-market}
\end{equation}

The aggregate resource constraint is derived rather than imposed as an
additional restriction. Substitute developer profit
\eqref{eq:axm-developer-profit}, labor-market clearing, and AI-service-market
clearing into the household budget \eqref{eq:axm-household-budget}:
\begin{equation}
 \dot K=rK+wL+p_XX-U-M-C.
 \label{eq:axm-capital-aggregation-step}
\end{equation}
Using the final sector's zero-profit identity
\eqref{eq:axm-final-zero-profit} yields
\begin{equation}
 C+\dot K+\delta K+U+M=Y.
 \label{eq:axm-resource}
\end{equation}

Only now can price-taking conditions from different agents be combined. The
household Euler equation is \(g_C=n+r-\rho\), while the final-good firm's capital
demand satisfies \(r=\alpha Y/K-\delta\) after capital-market clearing. Their
combination gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dot C}{C}=n+\alpha\frac{Y}{K}-\delta-\rho.
 \label{eq:axm-euler-reduced}
\end{equation}
Likewise, substituting the firm's capital-price condition into the developer's
costate equation gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dot q}{q}=\alpha\frac{Y}{K}-\delta-\frac{X}{qA^2}
 -\eta s_M\frac{\dot A}{A}.
 \label{eq:axm-costate-reduced}
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:axm-euler-reduced} and
\eqref{eq:axm-costate-reduced} are equilibrium conditions. They are not
first-order conditions of the household or the developer in isolation.

The model therefore contains two feedback loops in the terminology of
\citet{davidsonetal2026}. The technological loop is
\(A\to AM\to E\to\dot A\to A\). The economic loop is
\(A\to X\to Y\to(K,M)\to AM\to E\to\dot A\to A\).
Three local elasticities summarize the links that form these feedback loops:
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac{\partial\ln X}{\partial\ln A}\right|_U=1,\qquad
 \left.\frac{\partial\ln Y}{\partial\ln X}\right|_{K,L}
 =(1-\alpha)s_X,\qquad
 \left.\frac{\partial\ln\dot A}{\partial\ln(AM)}\right|_H=\eta s_M.
 \label{eq:axm-local-feedback-links}
\end{equation}
The strength of the full economic loop is not a primitive constant because
saving, compute allocation, labor allocation, and prices are determined in
equilibrium.

\begin{definition}[Admissible path]
An admissible path has measurable nonnegative controls, absolutely continuous
nonnegative stocks \(K\) and \(A\), the exogenous population path \(N\), the
stated initial stocks, and satisfies the
relevant flow budget and state equations. Its improper objective integrals
converge to finite real values. Deviations used to test household or developer
optimality must satisfy the corresponding requirements.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Interior equilibrium]
Given \(K(0),A(0),N(0)>0\), an interior equilibrium is an admissible allocation
with positive quantities and positive \(w\) and \(p_X\); the net interest rate
\(r\) may have either sign. The household solves its intertemporal problem
subject to \eqref{eq:axm-household-budget} and \(K\geq0\), competitive
final-good firms maximize profits, the integrated developer solves
\eqref{eq:axm-developer-problem}, and labor, capital, AI-service, and goods
markets clear.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Interior candidate path]
An interior candidate path has positive quantities and positive \(w,p_X,q\), and
satisfies the household Euler and flow-budget equations, the population law,
competitive factor demands, the monopoly first- and second-order conditions,
both research first-order conditions, the developer's state, costate, and
transversality condition, the household transversality condition, all technology
and service identities, and labor, capital, AI-service, and goods market clearing.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Equilibrium conditions and candidate-path characterization}

Every regular interior equilibrium necessarily satisfies the following dated
conditions. Together, the three blocks below characterize an interior candidate
path; they characterize a globally optimal equilibrium only under the conditions
of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-equilibrium-sufficiency}. The first block repeats
the dated conditions in the order of their source. In particular, the household
Euler equation and the developer costate remain in terms of the market return
\(r\) that each agent takes as given. The block is written for
\(\sigma_{XL}\neq1\) and
\(\sigma_{HM}\neq1\); at unit
elasticity, the corresponding CES and price-index equations are replaced by the
continuous limits stated above. Let \(e_X=\varepsilon_X^{-1}\).

\subsubsection{Dated equilibrium conditions by source}
\label{sec:axm-dated-equilibrium-system}
Because market clearing has now been imposed, the collected system uses the
single quantities \(K\) and \(X\). This notational consolidation occurs here,
not in the individual derivations above. The headings retain the source of each
condition before the conditions are combined.
\begingroup
\allowdisplaybreaks[4]
\begin{align}
\intertext{\textit{Exogenous process:}}
 \dot N&=nN,\\
\intertext{\textit{Household feasibility and optimality in prices:}}
 \dot K&=rK+wN+\Pi-C,\\
 \frac{\dot C}{C}&=n+r-\rho,\\
\intertext{\textit{Final-good firms:}}
 Y&=K^\alpha Z^{1-\alpha},\\
 Z&=\left[
 \omega_L L^{(\sigma_{XL}-1)/\sigma_{XL}}
 +\omega_X X^{(\sigma_{XL}-1)/\sigma_{XL}}
 \right]^{\sigma_{XL}/(\sigma_{XL}-1)},\\
 s_X&=\frac{\omega_X X^{(\sigma_{XL}-1)/\sigma_{XL}}}
 {\omega_LL^{(\sigma_{XL}-1)/\sigma_{XL}}
 +\omega_X X^{(\sigma_{XL}-1)/\sigma_{XL}}},\\
 r&=\alpha\frac{Y}{K}-\delta,\\
 w&=(1-\alpha)(1-s_X)\frac{Y}{L},\\
 p_X&=(1-\alpha)s_X\frac{Y}{X},\\
 Y&=(r+\delta)K+wL+p_XX,\\
 e_X&=\frac{1-s_X}{\sigma_{XL}}+\alpha s_X,\\
\intertext{\textit{AI-developer technologies, accounting, and optimality:}}
 X&=AU,\\
 E&=\left[\omega_HH^{\varrho_{HM}}

 \right]^{1/(1-\sigma_{HM})},\\

 H&=\omega_H^{\sigma_{HM}}
 \left(\frac{P_E}{w}\right)^{\sigma_{HM}}E,\\
 AM&=\omega_M^{\sigma_{HM}}(AP_E)^{\sigma_{HM}}E,\\

 q\frac{\partial\dot A}{\partial H}&=w,\\
 q\frac{\partial\dot A}{\partial M}&=1,\\
 \Pi&=p_XX-U-wH-M,\\
 \frac{\dot q}{q}&=r-\frac{X}{qA^2}

 N&=L+H,\\

\end{align}
\endgroup
The block is supplemented by the strict monopoly second-order condition
\eqref{eq:axm-monopoly-soc}. It deliberately displays equivalent
representations and audit identities, not additional independent restrictions.
The inverse-demand schedule was derived from final-good firms' optimal demand
before it was used in the developer's problem. Capital- and AI-service-market
clearing then identify the quantities denoted by \(K\) and \(X\) across those
separate problems; they do not add tautological equations to the collected
system.
The dual research conditions imply the two direct research first-order
conditions; the research price and both conditional demands imply the CES
identity for \(E\); and the final-firm first-order conditions imply zero profit.
After market clearing, the household budget, developer profit, final-firm zero
profit, and the resource constraint are also alternative representations of the
same aggregate accounting. Keeping them visible permits an equation-by-equation
audit of the reduced numerical system without obscuring their origin.

\subsubsection{Reduced dynamic system}

After imposing capital- and AI-service-market clearing, the independent
algebraic subsystem given \(K,A,N,q\) consists of labor
clearing; final production, shares, and prices; the service identity \(X=AU\);
inverse demand and the monopoly first-order condition; and the research price,
conditional demands, and scale first-order condition. This subsystem
characterizes a root on the selected interior branch for the candidate
quantities \(L,H,U,M,X,E,\) and \(Y\). It also determines the prices
\(r,w,p_X,P_E\) and the shares \(s_X,s_M\). The numerical solver computes an
interior root and retains it only if the ex-post audit also verifies the monopoly
second-order condition; no claim of global uniqueness is made.

The reduction now combines conditions that were kept separate in the individual
problems. The capital law uses the accounting derivation in
\eqref{eq:axm-capital-aggregation-step}--\eqref{eq:axm-resource}. The consumption
law substitutes the final-good firm's capital-price condition into the household
Euler equation, as shown in \eqref{eq:axm-euler-reduced}. The shadow-value law
makes the same price substitution in the developer costate, as shown in
\eqref{eq:axm-costate-reduced}. Together with the exogenous population law, the
reduced system is
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:axm-canonical-system}
\begin{align}
 \frac{\dot N}{N}&=n,\\
 \frac{\dot K}{K}&=\frac{Y-C-U-M}{K}-\delta,\\
 \frac{\dot A}{A}&=\frac{\chi E^\eta}{A},\\
 \frac{\dot C}{C}&=n+\alpha\frac{Y}{K}-\delta-\rho,\\
 \frac{\dot q}{q}
 &=\alpha\frac{Y}{K}-\delta-\frac{X}{qA^2}\notag\\
 &\quad-\eta s_M\frac{\dot A}{A}.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
\subsubsection{Initial and terminal conditions}

After capital-market clearing, \(K\) and \(A\) are predetermined, while the
population path is exogenous; \(C\) and \(q\) are jump variables. Initial
\(K,A,N\), market clearing, and the terminal restrictions close the system. The
household and developer transversality conditions were stated separately in
\eqref{eq:axm-household-tvc} and \eqref{eq:axm-developer-tvc}. After imposing
capital-market clearing and substituting the final-good firm's capital-price
condition into the developer's discount factor, they can be collected in
equilibrium as
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-\rho t}\frac{N_tK_t}{C_t}=0,\qquad
 \lim_{t\to\infty}
 \exp\left\{-\int_0^t\left(\alpha\frac{Y_s}{K_s}-\delta\right)\dd s\right\}
 q_tA_t=0.
\label{eq:axm-tvc}
\end{equation}
The first restriction applies to the household's capital holdings after market
clearing and the second to the developer's capability stock. This display does
not change either agent's original condition; it expresses both using equilibrium
quantities. For the reported unit-elasticity paths, the
numerical boundary conditions select transitions approaching analytical limits
that satisfy these restrictions under \(\rho>n\). A finite-horizon calculation
cannot itself verify an infinite-horizon transversality condition.

The dated first-order, state, market, and accounting conditions are necessary for
a regular interior equilibrium. The transversality conditions are maintained
terminal restrictions; their universal necessity is not asserted. The static
second-order condition verifies local monopoly optimality in \(X\), but the
paper does not establish global concavity of the developer's intertemporal
problem for all parameter values. Outside the sufficient cases stated next, a
solution of the displayed equations is therefore only a candidate path.

\begin{proposition}[Global sufficiency in the reported cases]
\label{prop:axm-equilibrium-sufficiency}
Let \(\sigma_{XL}=1\) and
\(\beta=(1-\alpha)\omega_X\leq1/2\). Suppose either
\[
 \sigma_{HM}=1,\qquad \eta(1+\omega_M)\leq1,
\]
or
\[
 \sigma_{HM}=2,\qquad 2\eta\leq1.
\]
Every regular interior equilibrium that satisfies the two stated transversality
conditions obeys the candidate system. Conversely, every admissible
infinite-horizon candidate path is an interior equilibrium.
\end{proposition}

The proof is in the Appendix. The benchmark satisfies these restrictions with
\(\beta=0.134\), \(\eta(1+\omega_M)=0.6075\), and \(2\eta=0.90\).
Thus the dated equations are necessary under the usual regularity conditions,
and the full system is globally sufficient for both reported unit-elasticity
parameterizations. We do not claim that the displayed transversality conditions
are universally necessary without additional infinite-horizon regularity. Nor
does the proposition turn a finite-horizon numerical approximation into a proof
that an infinite-horizon path exists or satisfies those limits.

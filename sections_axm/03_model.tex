\section{Model}

Time is continuous. Population grows at rate \(n\), and people work either in
final production or AI research:
\begin{equation}
 \dot N=nN,\qquad N=L+H.
 \label{eq:axm-labor-market}
\end{equation}
Here \(L\) always denotes production labor and \(H\) human research labor.
For any positive variable \(V\), write \(g_V\equiv\dot V/V\). Because both types
of labor earn the common wage \(w\), the total wage bill is \(wN=wL+wH\).

\begin{assumption}[Parameters]
Throughout, \(0<\alpha<1\), \(0<n<\rho\), \(\delta\geq0\),
\(\chi>0\), \(0<\eta<1\), \(\sigma_{XL},\sigma_{HM}>0\), and all CES
weights lie strictly between zero and one and sum to one within each CES
aggregate.
\end{assumption}

The no-AI economy is a useful boundary case, although it lies outside the
maintained interior-weight assumption. If \(\omega_X=\omega_M=0\), then
\(Z=L\), capability has no productive use, and the developer has no incentive to
conduct research. The model reduces to a Ramsey--Cass--Koopmans economy with
population growth and no technological progress.

\subsection{Household and final production}

The representative household maximizes
\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{-\rho t}N_t\log(C_t/N_t)\,\dd t
\]
and owns physical capital and the AI developer. It receives labor income, capital
income, and the developer's distributed profits. Its interior Euler equation in
aggregate variables is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dot C}{C}=n+\alpha\frac{Y}{K}-\delta-\rho.
 \label{eq:axm-euler}
\end{equation}
Competitive final-good firms produce
\begin{align}
 Y&=K^\alpha Z^{1-\alpha},\\
 Z&=\left[\omega_L L^{\frac{\sigma_{XL}-1}{\sigma_{XL}}}
 +\omega_X X^{\frac{\sigma_{XL}-1}{\sigma_{XL}}}\right]^{
 \frac{\sigma_{XL}}{\sigma_{XL}-1}},
 \qquad \omega_L+\omega_X=1.
 \label{eq:axm-final-production}
\end{align}
At the unit-elasticity parameter limit, \(\sigma_{XL}\to1\), the CES becomes the
Cobb--Douglas aggregate \(Z=L^{\omega_L}X^{\omega_X}\). Define the contribution
of AI services to the CES quantity index as
\begin{equation}
 s_X=\frac{\omega_X X^{(\sigma_{XL}-1)/\sigma_{XL}}}
 {\omega_L L^{(\sigma_{XL}-1)/\sigma_{XL}}
 +\omega_X X^{(\sigma_{XL}-1)/\sigma_{XL}}}.
 \label{eq:axm-sx}
\end{equation}
Thus \(s_X\) is the elasticity of \(Z\) with respect to \(X\), not AI services'
revenue share in final output. The latter is \(p_XX/Y=(1-\alpha)s_X\). Factor prices satisfy
\begin{equation}
 r=\alpha\frac{Y}{K}-\delta,\qquad
 w=(1-\alpha)(1-s_X)\frac{Y}{L},\qquad
 p_X=(1-\alpha)s_X\frac{Y}{X}.
 \label{eq:axm-factor-prices}
\end{equation}
Here \(r\) is the net real interest rate. The Euler equation above can therefore
be written as \(\dot C/C=n+r-\rho\).

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{remark}[Tasks, jobs, and labor services]
The inputs \(L\) and \(H\) are quantities of homogeneous labor services, not
numbers of tasks, occupations, or jobs. A job can combine several tasks, and AI
can replace some of them without eliminating the job. The model abstracts from
that margin. In addition, \(N=L+H\) imposes full employment: people can move
between final production and AI research, but the model contains neither
unemployment nor job creation and destruction. Consequently, \(\sigma_{XL}\)
measures substitution between aggregate labor and AI services in final
production. It is not a task-level elasticity or a direct measure of the number
of jobs displaced by AI. The model's labor-market predictions concern wages,
factor shares, and the allocation of labor between the two sectors.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Two uses of compute}

\(A\) indexes current AI capability: what one unit of compute can accomplish.
\(U\) is inference compute, and \(M\) is research compute. Inference compute
produces the service flow \(X=AU\). Analogously, research compute produces the
automated research service flow \(AM\).
The paid inputs are \(U\) and \(M\). Because both uses have the same constant unit
cost, we choose units of raw compute so that one unit costs one unit of final output. This is a
normalization, not an assumption that compute is economically free. To see this,
start from a common cost \(\xi>0\), define
\(\widetilde U=\xi U\), \(\widetilde M=\xi M\),
\(\widetilde A=A/\xi\), \(\widetilde\chi=\chi/\xi\), and, for the shadow
value used below, \(\widetilde q=\xi q\), and then drop tildes.
Both service flows and all resource costs are unchanged. Because one unit of raw
compute costs one unit of final output, producing one unit of either service
flow---one unit of \(X\) or of \(AM\)---uses \(1/A\) units of the final good. This separates the capability stock
\(A\), two raw compute inputs \(U,M\), and the two service flows \(X,AM\).

\subsection{AI research}

For \(\sigma_{HM}\neq1\), human researchers and automated research services
improve capability through a homothetic CES technology
\begin{equation}
 \dot A=\chi
 \left[\omega_H H^{\varrho_{HM}}
 +\omega_M(AM)^{\varrho_{HM}}\right]^{\eta/\varrho_{HM}},
 \qquad
 \varrho_{HM}=\frac{\sigma_{HM}-1}{\sigma_{HM}},
 \qquad \omega_H+\omega_M=1.
 \label{eq:axm-capability-law}
\end{equation}
For the dual representation used below, let \(E\) denote the constant-returns
effective-research index
\begin{equation}
 E\equiv\left[\omega_H H^{\varrho_{HM}}
 +\omega_M(AM)^{\varrho_{HM}}\right]^{1/\varrho_{HM}}.
 \label{eq:axm-effective-research}
\end{equation}
The object \(E\) is an index of the two research inputs, not an additional good,
stock, or production stage. The capability law is equivalently
\(\dot A=\chi E^\eta\); hence a target \(\dot A\) requires
\(E=(\dot A/\chi)^{1/\eta}\). At the unit-elasticity parameter limit,
\(\sigma_{HM}\to1\), equation \eqref{eq:axm-capability-law} becomes
\begin{equation}
 \dot A=\chi H^{\eta\omega_H}(AM)^{\eta\omega_M},
 \qquad E=H^{\omega_H}(AM)^{\omega_M}.
 \label{eq:axm-capability-law-cd}
\end{equation}

The parameters have separate economic roles. The elasticity \(\sigma_{HM}\)
governs substitution between human research services \(H\) and automated research
services \(AM\). The exponent \(\eta\) governs returns to their joint scale: for a
fixed \(A\), multiplying both \(H\) and \(M\) by \(\lambda\) multiplies
\(\dot A\) by \(\lambda^\eta\). The scalar \(\chi\) is multiplicative research
productivity and affects the level and timing of capability improvement. Finally, \(A\) enters
inside \(AM\) because capability raises the automated research services produced
by one unit of research compute. These roles are distinct: a high substitution
elasticity does not remove decreasing returns when \(0<\eta<1\).

The baseline contains no additional \(A^\phi\) term. Better capability affects
research only by raising the services produced by \(M\). This isolates research
automation without adding a separate standing-on-shoulders channel.

\begin{remark}[The constant-returns boundary]
\label{rem:axm-eta-one}
At \(\eta=1\), doubling \(H\) and \(M\) for a fixed capability stock doubles
\(\dot A\). If, in addition, \(\sigma_{HM}>1\) and \(AM/H\to\infty\), then
\[
 \frac{\dot A}{A}
 \sim \chi\,\omega_M^{1/\varrho_{HM}}M.
\]
In this technological limit, a higher level of research compute raises the growth
rate of capability. This scaling does not by itself establish which equilibrium
branch is reached or whether output becomes singular because \(M\) is endogenous.
Moreover, the interior scale condition becomes \(q\chi=P_E\) and no longer
determines \(E\). If \(q\chi<P_E\), the developer chooses no research at the
margin; if \(q\chi>P_E\), its unconstrained problem has no finite interior
research choice. We maintain
\(0<\eta<1\) in the baseline to distinguish substitution from research-scale
effects. The boundary \(\eta=1\) requires a separate analysis of interiority,
existence, transversality, and the asymptotic equilibrium.
\end{remark}

For \(\sigma_{HM}\neq1\), the unit expenditure function of the
effective-research index \(E\) is
\begin{equation}
 P_E=\left[\omega_H^{\sigma_{HM}}w^{1-\sigma_{HM}}
 +\omega_M^{\sigma_{HM}}\left(\frac{1}{A}\right)^{1-\sigma_{HM}}
 \right]^{1/(1-\sigma_{HM})}.
 \label{eq:axm-research-price}
\end{equation}
At the unit-elasticity parameter limit, \(\sigma_{HM}\to1\), its continuous limit is
\begin{equation}
 P_E=\left(\frac{w}{\omega_H}\right)^{\omega_H}
 \left(\frac{1}{A\omega_M}\right)^{\omega_M}.
 \label{eq:axm-research-price-cd}
\end{equation}
Conditional on current \(A\) and \(w\), and because
\(E=(\dot A/\chi)^{1/\eta}\), the minimum expenditure required to produce a
target flow \(\dot A\) is
\begin{equation}
 P_E\left(\frac{\dot A}{\chi}\right)^{1/\eta}.
 \label{eq:axm-research-expenditure-function}
\end{equation}
Thus \(P_E\) is not the unit cost of \(\dot A\) when \(\eta<1\).
Conditional input demands are
\begin{equation}
 H=\omega_H^{\sigma_{HM}}\left(\frac{P_E}{w}\right)^{\sigma_{HM}}E,
 \quad
 AM=\omega_M^{\sigma_{HM}}
 \left(AP_E\right)^{\sigma_{HM}}E.
 \label{eq:axm-research-demands}
\end{equation}
The share of research expenditure allocated to automated research services is
\begin{equation}
 s_M=\frac{M}{wH+M}.
 \label{eq:axm-sm}
\end{equation}
Because producing \(AM\) costs \(M\), the conditional demands imply that, along
an interior cost-minimizing research allocation, \(s_M\) is also the elasticity
of \(E\) with respect to \(AM\). It follows that the elasticity of \(\dot A\)
with respect to \(AM\) is \(\eta s_M\). Holding \(H\) and \(M\) fixed, this is
also the local elasticity of \(\dot A\) with respect to the predetermined
capability stock \(A\). These equalities use interior cost minimization; the
share \(s_M\) is not a physical fraction of research tasks performed by AI.

\subsection{Integrated AI developer}

The developer sells \(X\) to final-good firms, pays \(X/A\) for inference
compute, hires \(H\), uses \(M\) internally, and owns \(A\). Its market power is
confined to the sale of \(X\): the developer takes the wage, the common unit cost
of raw compute, and the final sector's current \(K\) and \(L\) as given. Its
market power is therefore exercised against the inverse demand for \(X\)
conditional on those two inputs. Labor-market clearing is imposed in equilibrium,
but the developer does not internalize its effect on the competitive wage. Its
intertemporal problem is
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\{X,H,M\}}
 \int_0^\infty
 \exp\left\{-\int_0^t r_s\dd s\right\}
 \left[p_{X,t}X_t-\frac{X_t}{A_t}-w_tH_t-M_t\right]\dd t.
 \label{eq:axm-developer-problem}
\end{equation}
The maximization is subject to the capability law
\eqref{eq:axm-capability-law} and the initial capability stock.
The discount rate is the return available to the household that owns the
developer. Along an interior equilibrium, this discounting is consistent with
the household Euler equation. The inverse of the
absolute price elasticity of demand for \(X\) is
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_X^{-1}=\frac{1-s_X}{\sigma_{XL}}+\alpha s_X.
 \label{eq:axm-inverse-elasticity}
\end{equation}
On an interior monopoly branch,
\begin{equation}
 p_X(1-\varepsilon_X^{-1})=\frac{1}{A}.
 \label{eq:axm-monopoly-foc}
\end{equation}
Writing \(e_X=\varepsilon_X^{-1}\), the strict second-order condition for the
static supply choice is
\begin{equation}
 e_X(1-e_X)
 +(\alpha-\sigma_{XL}^{-1})(1-\sigma_{XL}^{-1})s_X(1-s_X)>0.
 \label{eq:axm-monopoly-soc}
\end{equation}
The numerical solver checks this inequality at every reported date.
Let \(q\) be the current-value shadow price of capability. Research optimality is
\begin{equation}
 q\frac{\partial\dot A}{\partial H}=w,\qquad
 q\frac{\partial\dot A}{\partial M}=1,
 \label{eq:axm-research-focs}
\end{equation}
or, after minimizing the cost of the effective-research index \(E\),
\begin{equation}
 q\chi\eta E^{\eta-1}=P_E.
 \label{eq:axm-research-scale-foc}
\end{equation}
Let \(\pi(A;K,L)\) denote optimized operating profit from selling \(X\), before
research expenditure. Holding \(K\) and \(L\) fixed, its envelope derivative is
\(\pi_A=X/A^2\). Along the
interior cost-minimizing research allocation,
\(\partial\dot A/\partial A=\eta s_M\dot A/A\) because a fixed \(M\) supplies
more research services when \(A\) rises. The costate equation is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dot q}{q}=\alpha\frac{Y}{K}-\delta-\frac{X}{qA^2}
 -\eta s_M\frac{\dot A}{A}.
 \label{eq:axm-costate}
\end{equation}
The last term is the main dynamic difference from research that combines \(H\)
directly with \(M\).

\subsection{Resources and equilibrium}

The resource constraint is
\begin{equation}
 C+\dot K+\delta K+U+M=Y.
 \label{eq:axm-resource}
\end{equation}

Let distributed developer profit be
\[
 \Pi=p_XX-U-wH-M.
\]
The household flow budget is
\begin{equation}
 \dot K=rK+wN+\Pi-C.
 \label{eq:axm-household-budget}
\end{equation}
Competitive final-good firms earn zero profit, so
\(Y=(r+\delta)K+wL+p_XX\). Using \(N=L+H\), the household budget is
exactly equivalent to the aggregate resource constraint
\eqref{eq:axm-resource}.

\begin{definition}[Admissible path]
An admissible path has measurable nonnegative controls, absolutely continuous
nonnegative stocks \(K\) and \(A\), the stated initial stocks, and satisfies the
relevant flow budget and state equations. Its improper objective integrals
converge to finite real values. Deviations used to test household or developer
optimality must satisfy the corresponding requirements.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Interior equilibrium]
Given \(K(0),A(0),N(0)>0\), an interior equilibrium is an admissible allocation
with positive quantities and positive \(w\) and \(p_X\); the net interest rate
\(r\) may have either sign. The household solves its intertemporal
problem subject to \eqref{eq:axm-household-budget} and \(K\geq0\), competitive
final-good firms maximize profits, the integrated developer solves
\eqref{eq:axm-developer-problem}, and labor, capital, AI-service, and goods markets
clear.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Interior candidate path]
An interior candidate path has positive quantities and positive \(w,p_X,q\), and
satisfies the household Euler and
flow-budget equations, the population law, competitive factor demands, the
monopoly first- and second-order conditions, both research first-order conditions,
the developer's state, costate, and transversality conditions, all technology and
service identities, and labor and goods market clearing.
\end{definition}

More explicitly, the candidate system comprises: (i) the population and labor
market equation \eqref{eq:axm-labor-market}, final production
\eqref{eq:axm-final-production}, the CES share \eqref{eq:axm-sx}, factor prices
\eqref{eq:axm-factor-prices}, and \(X=AU\); (ii) the
capability law \eqref{eq:axm-capability-law}, or its unit-elasticity limit, and the
dual research conditions \eqref{eq:axm-research-price}--\eqref{eq:axm-sm};
(iii) monopoly conditions \eqref{eq:axm-monopoly-foc}--
\eqref{eq:axm-monopoly-soc}, research conditions
\eqref{eq:axm-research-focs}--\eqref{eq:axm-research-scale-foc}, and the costate
equation \eqref{eq:axm-costate}; and (iv) the household budget
\eqref{eq:axm-household-budget}, resource constraint \eqref{eq:axm-resource},
Euler equation \eqref{eq:axm-euler}, initial stocks, and the transversality
conditions below. Some equations are deliberately redundant: the dual research
conditions imply the two direct research FOCs, while the household budget and
resource constraint coincide after market clearing. The audit checks both the
reduced computational block and these primitive identities.

The dated first-order, state, market, and accounting conditions are necessary for
a regular interior equilibrium. The transversality conditions are maintained
terminal restrictions; their universal necessity is not asserted. The static
second-order condition verifies local monopoly optimality in \(X\), but the paper
does not establish global concavity of the developer's intertemporal problem for
all parameter values. Outside the sufficient cases stated next, a solution of the
equations is therefore only a candidate path.

\begin{proposition}[Global sufficiency in the reported cases]
\label{prop:axm-equilibrium-sufficiency}
Let \(\sigma_{XL}=1\) and
\(\beta=(1-\alpha)\omega_X\leq1/2\). Suppose either
\[
 \sigma_{HM}=1,\qquad \eta(1+\omega_M)\leq1,
\]
or
\[
 \sigma_{HM}=2,\qquad 2\eta\leq1.
\]
Every regular interior equilibrium that satisfies the two stated transversality
conditions obeys the candidate system. Conversely, every admissible
infinite-horizon candidate path is an interior equilibrium.
\end{proposition}

The proof is in the Appendix. The benchmark satisfies these restrictions with
\(\beta=0.134\), \(\eta(1+\omega_M)=0.6075\), and \(2\eta=0.90\).
Thus the dated equations are necessary under the usual regularity conditions and
the full system is globally sufficient for both reported unit-elasticity
parameterizations. We
do not claim that the displayed transversality conditions are universally
necessary without additional infinite-horizon regularity. Nor does the
proposition turn a finite-horizon numerical approximation into a proof that an
infinite-horizon path exists or satisfies those limits.

The system used for computation is explicit. Given \(K,A,N,q\), equations
\eqref{eq:axm-labor-market} and
\eqref{eq:axm-final-production}--\eqref{eq:axm-research-scale-foc} characterize
candidate static values of \(L,H,U,M,X,E,Y,w,p_X,P_E,s_X,\) and \(s_M\), where
\(E\) is defined in \eqref{eq:axm-effective-research}. The numerical solver
computes an interior root; a reported root is retained only if the ex-post audit
also verifies the monopoly second-order condition. Together
with \(\dot N/N=n\), the endogenous dynamics are
\begin{align}
 \frac{\dot K}{K}&=\frac{Y-C-U-M}{K}-\delta,\\
 \frac{\dot A}{A}&=\frac{\chi E^\eta}{A},\\
 \frac{\dot C}{C}&=n+\alpha\frac{Y}{K}-\delta-\rho,\\
 \frac{\dot q}{q}&=\alpha\frac{Y}{K}-\delta-\frac{X}{qA^2}
 -\eta s_M\frac{\dot A}{A}.
 \label{eq:axm-canonical-system}
\end{align}
\(K\) and \(A\) are predetermined; \(C\) and \(q\) are jump variables selected
by the terminal conditions. The block is closed by initial \(K,A,N\), the labor
and goods market identities, and the transversality conditions displayed below.
In current-value
notation, the relevant terminal restrictions include
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-\rho t}\frac{N_tK_t}{C_t}=0,\qquad
 \lim_{t\to\infty}
 \exp\left\{-\int_0^t\left(\alpha\frac{Y_s}{K_s}-\delta\right)\dd s\right\}
 q_tA_t=0.
\label{eq:axm-tvc}
\end{equation}
The first restriction applies to household capital and the second to the
developer's capability stock. For the reported unit-elasticity paths, the
numerical boundary conditions select transitions approaching analytical limits
that satisfy these restrictions under \(\rho>n\). The finite-horizon calculation
cannot itself verify an infinite-horizon transversality condition.

\begin{definition}[Balanced growth and singularity]
A balanced-growth path is a path on which the growth rates of all nonstationary
variables converge to finite constants and allocation shares converge to
constants. An accelerating path is one on which the output growth rate eventually
rises without bound; other nonconvergent paths are not classified as accelerating
by this definition. Following \citet{aghionjonesjones2019}, a
finite-time singularity occurs when output becomes unbounded at a finite date.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Pre-singular candidate branch]
A pre-singular candidate branch is a solution of the dated necessary conditions
on its maximal interval \([0,T^*)\), where \(T^*<\infty\), that remains interior
at every finite date and approaches a finite-time singularity as
\(t\uparrow T^*\). It is not, by that label alone, an infinite-horizon equilibrium:
global intertemporal optimality and an allocation at or after \(T^*\) require
additional analysis.
\end{definition}
